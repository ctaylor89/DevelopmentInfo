I do know QBDT does have a general chart of accounts and one can search “standard chart of account. The other place to start 
is the standard numbering structure in accounting.

10000 - 19999    Assets (what the business owns, such as inventory, bank accounts) 
20000 - 29999   Liabilities (what the business owes, credit cards, loans, payroll)        
30000 - 39999   Equity (Owner’s capital and withdrawals or net worth of business)   
40000 - 49999   Revenue (Income from product or service sales)
50000 - 59999    Cost of Goods Sold (items used to make a product or goods for resale)
60000 - 99999  Expenses (Bills - office supplies, advertising, phone, etc.)

This is definitely the way to start! The standard Chart of Accounts that QBO (Online) or QBD (Desktop) are not always the best 
move. When I bring on a new client that has NEVER had bookkeeping, I import a standard chart of accounts when I sign them up for 
the subscription. This avoids QB assigning it based on their industry.

So many people don’t fully reconcile before they send statements. 
Now the truth is you might not be able to reconcile a full month if you are missing a document but you complete what you can, send reports and include a disclaimer that the books aren’t finalized because you a missing “x, y or z”. 
---
Once I have reconciled the statements, I print a physical copy and run down each figure. I run the corresponding report to make sure the Balance Sheet figure matches. 

For example, for a cash or credit account balance, I run the account reconciliation. For receivables, I run the A/R, etc. If the figures do not match, I know there is a problem that needs to be investigated. The Balance Sheet is a General Ledger report while the corresponding reports are sub ledgers looking at transactional data from sources other than the GL.

This process gives me confidence in my work and in the presentation of statements to my clients.
